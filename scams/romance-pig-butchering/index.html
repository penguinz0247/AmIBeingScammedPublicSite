Romance & “pig-butchering” investment scam

Online relationships that turn into requests for money or crypto — the warning signs, and what to do.

What it looks like

It often starts warmly: a friendly message, a “wrong number” text that turns into a chat, or a new match who seems perfect. Over days or weeks they build trust and affection. They always have a reason they can’t meet in person or on a live video call.

Then the ask comes. It might be an “emergency,” a request for gift cards, or an invitation to a “can’t-lose” crypto investment on an app or website they recommend. The investment looks like it’s growing — until you try to withdraw and are told to pay “taxes” or “fees” first.

The warning signs

  • A stranger online who grows very close, very fast.
  • They won’t meet in person or on a live video call.
  • They steer you to a crypto or investment app they picked.
  • Returns that look amazing — and a “tax” or “fee” to withdraw.
  • Any request for money, gift cards, or crypto.

What to do

  • Never send money, gift cards, or crypto to someone you’ve only met online.
  • Be very cautious with any investment a new online friend recommends.
  • Stop contact if money comes up. Talk to a friend or relative you trust.
  • It’s okay to walk away. Real relationships don’t require a payment.

How to check for yourself

  • Try a live video call. Scammers will keep making excuses.
  • Search the photos they sent (a reverse image search) — they’re often stolen.
  • Ask someone you trust to look at the messages with you.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each contact yourself before you use it.

If you already sent money or invested, see What to do if you paid or shared information. Many people are caught this way — it is not your fault.
