“Family emergency” call (grandparent & voice-clone) scam

What these calls sound like, the warning signs, and what to do next.

What it looks like

You get a call saying a loved one — a grandchild, a child, a niece or nephew — is in trouble. They’ve been in an accident, arrested, or stranded. The caller sounds upset and rushed.

They beg you not to tell anyone and to send money fast, often through gift cards, a wire transfer, or a courier who will come to your door. Some scammers now use recordings or voice-copying to sound like the real person.

The warning signs

  • A sudden call about a relative in trouble, with no warning.
  • A plea to keep it secret from the rest of the family.
  • A demand for money right now, before you can think.
  • Payment by gift cards, wire transfer, crypto, or a person sent to collect cash.
  • A voice that sounds “off,” or that won’t answer simple personal questions.

What to do

  • Don’t send money or buy gift cards because of a phone call.
  • Don’t keep it secret — that rule is the scam.
  • Take a breath. It’s okay to stop and check before you do anything.
  • Hang up and call your loved one yourself.

How to check for yourself

  • Call the family member directly, on the number you already have for them.
  • Call another relative who would know where that person is.
  • Ask a question only the real person could answer.
  • Consider agreeing on a family “safe word” now, for the future.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts — including support lines for older adults — in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each number yourself before you call.

If you already sent money, see What to do if you paid or shared information. It is not your fault, and acting quickly can help.
