I think I already got scammed

Calm, step-by-step help — and the people who can support you.

First, take a breath

If you think you were scammed, you are not alone, and it is not your fault. Scammers do this for a living. They are skilled at pressure and fear, and they fool careful, smart people every single day.

What matters now is what you do next. Acting soon can limit the damage. Let’s go step by step.

Start with the recovery steps

What to do if you paid or shared information

The most important thing is speed: for wires and bank transfers, calling your bank within hours can be the difference between a freeze and a permanent loss.

Report it

Reporting helps investigators and can warn others — you don’t need every detail to file. See Who to report a scam to for where to go, and remember to look up each contact yourself.

You deserve support

Being scammed can feel awful — embarrassed, angry, shaken. Those feelings are normal, and they pass. There are free, confidential, judgement-free helplines listed in the reporting guide, including support specifically for older adults. If the stress feels like too much to carry, please reach out to a crisis line — you deserve support.
