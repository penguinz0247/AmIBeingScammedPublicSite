Tech-support pop-up or call scam

What these fake virus warnings look like, the warning signs, and what to do.

What it looks like

A pop-up fills your screen and says your computer is infected or has been “blocked.” It may flash warnings, play a sound, or show a number to call “Microsoft” or “Apple” for help.

Sometimes it comes as a phone call instead. Either way, the person who answers wants to take control of your computer, charge you for a fake fix, or get into your bank account “to check it.”

The warning signs

  • A pop-up or call claiming your device is infected or hacked.
  • A phone number to call for “support” that you didn’t look up.
  • A request to install software so they can control your screen.
  • Pressure to pay for a fix with gift cards, wire transfer, or a payment app.
  • Being asked to log in to your bank while they watch.

What to do

  • Don’t call the number on the pop-up.
  • Don’t let anyone connect to or control your computer.
  • Don’t pay with gift cards, wire transfer, crypto, or a payment app.
  • Close the pop-up. If you can’t, shut the device down and restart it.
  • Real companies don’t warn you about viruses with a pop-up like this.

How to check for yourself

  • If you’re worried about your device, contact the maker (Microsoft, Apple, or your computer’s brand) through their official website.
  • Take the device to a local repair shop you trust.
  • Ask a family member or friend you trust to look with you.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each phone number or web address yourself before you use it.

If you paid or let someone into your computer, see What to do if you paid or shared information — disconnect the device from the internet first, then follow the steps there.
