Government imposter (SSA, IRS, police) scam

Calls and texts that pretend to be the government — the warning signs, and what to do.

What it looks like

You get a call or text that claims to be from the Social Security Administration, the IRS, a court, or the police. It says your Social Security number is “suspended,” you owe back taxes, you missed jury duty, or a warrant is out for your arrest.

The caller is forceful. They threaten arrest within the hour, tell you to stay on the line, and demand that you confirm your details or “fix” it by paying — often with gift cards, a wire, crypto, or by moving your money to a “safe account.”

The warning signs

  • An unexpected call or text threatening arrest, fines, or losing your benefits.
  • A claim that your Social Security number was “suspended” (that can’t happen).
  • Pressure to stay on the phone and act this minute.
  • A demand to pay by gift card, wire, crypto, or to move money to a “safe” account.
  • A request to confirm your SSN, bank, or Medicare number.

What to do

  • Hang up. Real agencies don’t threaten arrest or demand payment by phone.
  • Don’t pay, and don’t move your money because someone told you to.
  • Don’t share your Social Security, bank, or Medicare number.
  • Government agencies contact you first by mail — not with a threatening call.

How to check for yourself

  • Look up the agency’s real number on its official .gov website — not the number that called you.
  • Call the agency directly to ask if anything is really wrong.
  • Check with a family member or friend you trust before doing anything.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right agency in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each contact yourself before you use it.

If you already paid or shared details, see What to do if you paid or shared information — it is not your fault, and acting quickly can help.
