Bank or Amazon “fraud alert” scam

What these fake alerts look like, the warning signs, and what to do next.

What it looks like

You get a call, text, or email that looks like it’s from your bank or from Amazon. It says there’s a suspicious charge or a problem with your account. It wants you to act fast to “stop” it.

The catch comes next. They ask you to “verify” yourself by reading back a code they just texted you, sharing your login, or moving your money to a “safe account.” Any of these hands control straight to the scammer.

The warning signs

  • An alert you weren’t expecting about a charge you don’t recognize.
  • Pressure to act immediately to “protect” your account.
  • A request to read back a code that was texted to you.
  • A request for your password, PIN, or full account number.
  • Being told to move money to a “safe” or “holding” account.

What to do

  • Don’t reply, don’t click links, and don’t call the number they gave you.
  • Never read a verification code out loud or type it into a link someone sent.
  • Never move your money because someone on the phone told you to.
  • Hang up. A real bank is fine with you calling them back.

How to check for yourself

  • Find your bank’s phone number on the back of your card or on a statement — not from the message.
  • Call that number yourself and ask if there’s really a problem.
  • For Amazon, log in through the app or the website you normally use and check your orders and messages there.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each phone number or web address yourself before you use it.

If you shared any account details, codes, or moved money, see What to do if you paid or shared information right away — and contact your bank using the number on your card.
