Prize, sweepstakes & advance-fee scam

“You won!” messages that ask you to pay first — the warning signs, and what to do.

What it looks like

You’re told you’ve won — a sweepstakes, a lottery, a car, or a big cash prize — often one you don’t remember entering. The message is exciting and urgent.

To collect, they say you must first pay a “tax,” “handling,” or “processing” fee. Or they mail you a check, tell you to deposit it, and ask you to send part of it back. The check later bounces, and the money you sent is gone.

The warning signs

  • “Winning” a prize or lottery you never entered.
  • Being asked to pay a fee, tax, or shipping to receive a “free” prize.
  • A check you’re told to deposit and partly send back.
  • Pressure to act fast and keep it quiet.
  • Payment requested by gift card, wire, or a payment app.

What to do

  • Don’t pay anything to receive a prize. Real prizes are free.
  • Don’t deposit a check from a stranger, and never wire back “extra” funds.
  • Slow down. A real prize won’t vanish if you take a day to check.

How to check for yourself

  • Ask: did I actually enter this? If not, it isn’t real.
  • Look up the company or lottery yourself and call to confirm — don’t use their number.
  • Check with a family member or friend you trust before paying anything.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each contact yourself before you use it.
