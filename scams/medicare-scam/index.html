Medicare card & “free benefits” scam

Unsolicited Medicare calls and texts — the warning signs, and what to do.

What it looks like

You get a call or text out of the blue about Medicare. It might say a new plastic Medicare card is ready, offer a “free” grocery or flex card worth thousands, or ask you to “confirm” your details for new equipment like a back brace.

To continue, they ask for your Medicare number, Social Security number, or bank details. Real benefits don’t work this way, and your Medicare number is valuable to thieves.

The warning signs

  • An unsolicited Medicare call, text, or DM you didn’t ask for.
  • An offer of “free” groceries, cash, or equipment with no questions about your health.
  • A fee or payment to receive a card that should be free.
  • A request for your Medicare number, SSN, or bank details.
  • Pressure to act before an “enrollment deadline.”

What to do

  • Don’t give your Medicare number, SSN, or bank details to an unexpected caller.
  • Hang up. Medicare won’t call out of the blue to sell you a plan or hand out free benefits.
  • Treat your Medicare number like a credit card number.

How to check for yourself

  • Call Medicare yourself using the number on the back of your Medicare card.
  • Check your benefits by logging in at the official Medicare website you already use.
  • Ask a family member or friend you trust to look with you.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each phone number yourself before you call.

If you already shared your Medicare number or other details, see What to do if you paid or shared information.
