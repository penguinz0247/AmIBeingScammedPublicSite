Fake USPS / delivery “package held” text

What these delivery texts look like, the warning signs, and what to do next.

What it looks like

You get a text saying a package can’t be delivered. It might claim the address is wrong, a small fee is owed, or the package is “held” at a facility. It uses the name of USPS, UPS, FedEx, or another carrier.

The text includes a link to “fix” the problem. The link leads to a fake site that asks for your address, card number, or a small payment. That information is then stolen or used for more scams.

The warning signs

  • A delivery text when you aren’t expecting a package.
  • A request for a small fee to “release” a package.
  • A link that isn’t the carrier’s real website.
  • Spelling mistakes, odd phrasing, or a sense of rush.
  • A message asking you to “confirm” personal details.

What to do

  • Don’t click the link or call a number from the text.
  • Don’t pay a fee or enter card details from a page you reached through a text.
  • Don’t share your address or personal information.
  • Delete the message, or report it first.

How to check for yourself

  • Find your tracking number from the order confirmation you got directly from the seller.
  • Go to the carrier’s official website or app yourself and paste the tracking number there.
  • If something seems off, contact the seller or carrier using contact details from their real site.

Where to report it and get help

You can report this and find the right contacts in Who to report a scam to. Always look up each phone number or web address yourself before you use it.

If you already paid or shared something, see What to do if you paid or shared information — acting quickly can limit the damage, and it is not your fault.
