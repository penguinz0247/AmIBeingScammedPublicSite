Accessibility

Our commitment to making this site usable for everyone.

Plain starter text — have it reviewed before launch. Not legal advice.

Our commitment

This site is built to be usable by as many people as possible, including people who use screen readers, keyboards, magnification, or other assistive technology. We aim to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA, and we test against the newer WCAG 2.2 AA criteria as well. We intend this site to be accessible within the meaning of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

What we’ve done

  • Large, readable text and high-contrast colors.
  • Big tap targets (at least 44 by 44 pixels) with comfortable spacing.
  • Full keyboard support, with a visible focus outline at least 2 pixels thick.
  • No drag-only interactions, and nothing that requires precise pointer moves.
  • Clear headings and labels, and an early “skip to main content” link.
  • A help block with human support lines in the same place on every page.
  • Respect for your “reduce motion” setting — no surprise animation, no flashing, and no timed countdowns anywhere.
  • A checker that works one question at a time, with each new question announced to screen readers, and answers shown as large buttons — never dropdown menus.
  • Nothing to remember and nothing to retype: the site has no accounts, no passwords, and never asks for the same information twice.

How we check

Automated accessibility checks (axe, pa11y, and Lighthouse) run on every change before it can be published. Automated checks don’t catch everything, so we also review changes by hand against the guidelines above.

If something doesn’t work for you

We want to fix it. If you run into an accessibility barrier — something hard to read, hard to reach by keyboard, or unclear with a screen reader — please tell us through the contact method listed at launch. Let us know the page and what happened, and we’ll do our best to put it right.
